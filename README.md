## Why this line

Candidates tested against the judge criteria (genuine earned pride first,
then concrete scene, earned inside the line, landing in one read, freshness):

- The rose / watering-can line lost: the pride stayed in a metaphor (a flower
  standing up) instead of in the person doing the hard thing.
- The oak-chair line lost: forty winters of sanding is a long effort, but the
  payoff lands on the granddaughter sitting down, not on the maker.
- The chosen bike line wins: three summers of falling is felt effort, June
  and her father are seen in a concrete place, and the pride arrives only
  in the last two words, when his hand lets go.

## Critic round 1 — what changed and why

The bike line was rejected on three counts: the image is stock (a child
learning to ride while a parent lets go has been seen a hundred times), the
payoff belonged to the father rather than June, and "three summers of
falling off" summarises the difficulty instead of letting the reader feel
it. The replacement keeps the structure (named small person + long effort
+ concrete place + payoff in the final words) but changes the skill to a
pottery wheel: the difficulty is felt in-scene (the pot wobbling, then
collapsing, then rising straight), the payoff is June's own (she stares at
it, grinning, afraid to blink), and there is no front-loaded setup clause
to assemble — the scene starts with her hands on the clay.
